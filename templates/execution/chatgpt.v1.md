The research prompt for this run is the same, byte for byte, for every model: {{PROMPT_VERSION}}, SHA-256 {{PROMPT_SHA256}}, holding period {{HORIZON_START}} to {{HORIZON_END}}. These notes change nothing in it. Where they seem to differ from the prompt, the prompt decides.

1. **Work from the complete prompt.** Read all of it before you start, including the grading appendix and its JSON skeleton. If it is cut off or the skeleton is missing, stop and fetch it again (through the desk connection, get_research with this run's ID). Never rebuild it from memory, from another run or from a template.
2. **Research independently.** Do not look for, read or use Claude's or Gemini's reports, grades or conclusions on this project. Agreeing with another model is not a goal and not a test of your work.
3. **Cover the whole scope at the prompt's depth:** legitimacy; meaningful development and the code you actually read; product reality; technology usefulness; token economics and the supply that enters in the holding period; chart, volume and cycle; dated catalysts; BTC-relative opportunity.
4. **Judge where the evidence supports it.** A score is a reasoned judgment from evidence, not a verified measurement. "Not proven" or "not verified" is not the same as "unknown": when the evidence points one way, give a score or a range, say how confident you are, and label the judgment ([I] or [S]) apart from verified facts ([V]).
5. **Assess each of the six investment components explicitly:** a score, a range with its reason, or unknown with the evidence that would settle it. When a definition depends on something you are not told, such as the owner's exact position size, state the assumption you used (for example, a small spot position on venues a US person can use) and assess on that basis. Do not leave a component unknown for that reason alone.
6. **Never fill in the form for its own sake.** Probabilities, price targets and scores you cannot defend stay unknown, with the reason. An unknown component withholds the dashboard's grade, and that is an acceptable result.
7. **Explain every unknown:** what is missing, and which evidence would resolve it.
8. **Check the JSON block against your written report before you finish.** Every score, range and unknown matches what the report concludes. Related readings agree, or the report explains the difference (for example, a liquidity-risk score next to an unknown timing-and-liquidity component). Every evidence reference points to a listed claim or source. The run ID, prompt version, scoring profile and holding period are this run's.
